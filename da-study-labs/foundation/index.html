Introduction

What is data analytics?

Data analytics is “the collection, transformation, and organization” of facts so that people can draw conclusions, make predictions and make better decisions.Paraphrasing Google’s Data Analytics course, Introducing Data Analytics and Analytical Thinking

The point is the decision, not the dashboard. Data becomes useful when it moves up a ladder: raw facts are organised into information, information is interpreted into an insight, and the insight changes what someone does.

The analysis process

Google’s course teaches six phases that repeat as you learn more. CRISP-DM, the long-standing industry standard for data-mining projects, has the same shape: business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modelling, evaluation and deployment. The tag under each phase shows which concept on this page it uses most.

Types of data analytics

Four questions, each harder and more valuable

The four types are often drawn as a staircase, the “analytics ascendancy model” commonly attributed to Gartner: each step builds on the one before it, takes more effort, and moves closer to a decision. Most organisations still spend most of their time on the first step.

Where the definitions come from

  1. Google Career Certificates: Introducing Data Analytics and Analytical Thinking (Coursera). Definition of data analytics and the six phases: ask, prepare, process, analyze, share, act.
  2. Chapman et al., CRISP-DM 1.0: Step-by-step data mining guide (2000) The six CRISP-DM phases and the data understanding and preparation tasks.
  3. UK Government Data Quality Hub: Meet the data quality dimensions Accuracy, completeness, uniqueness, consistency, timeliness and validity, as defined by DAMA UK.
  4. NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods: What is EDA? Exploratory data analysis as an approach that is mostly graphical and lets the data reveal its structure. The full handbook covers the statistics used here.
  5. Office for National Statistics: Data visualisation guidance Principles, chart types, colour, axes and chart text.
  6. Google for Developers: Machine Learning Crash Course Supervised learning, loss, model evaluation and data preparation.
  7. Coursera: Descriptive, predictive and prescriptive analytics The questions each type answers, with examples.
  8. Moesmann and Pedersen, Data-Driven Prescriptive Analytics Applications: A Comprehensive Survey (2024) The analytics ascendancy model attributed to Gartner and definitions of predictive and prescriptive analytics.
