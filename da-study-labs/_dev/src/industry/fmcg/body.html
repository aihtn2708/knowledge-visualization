Retail Demand Forecasting Lab

A category planner at an FMCG company uses national retail sales as the demand signal for the coming months. Find the seasonal pattern, test three forecasting methods on data they have not seen, and turn the best forecast into a supply plan with a safety buffer.

facebook/prophet ★ about 20.4kMIT licencePython and R

Prophet is Meta's open-source forecasting library. This lab uses examples/example_retail_sales.csv from the repository: US Census Bureau monthly retail and food services sales, in millions of US dollars, January 1992 to May 2016. Prophet's own forecast is included for comparison.

Open the repository

The business question

DecisionHow much stock to plan for each of the next six months.
Why it mattersToo little stock loses sales during peaks; too much ties up cash and warehouse space after them.
Measure of successMean absolute percentage error (MAPE): the average size of the forecast miss, in percent of actual sales.
Data
Descriptive · what happened?

Twenty-four years of monthly sales

Two patterns are visible at once: a long upward trend and a saw-tooth that repeats every year. One dip breaks the trend. The next section separates the two patterns.

Diagnostic · why does it move?

Separate the season from the trend

Seasonal index by month

Sales in each month divided by the 12-month centred moving average, averaged over the training years. 1.00 is an average month.

Year-on-year growth

Comparing each month with the same month a year earlier removes the season, so changes in the trend show up clearly.

Predictive · what will happen?

Backtest three methods on the last 24 months

Hide the last 24 months (June 2014 to May 2016), fit each method on everything before, and measure the miss. This is the only honest way to compare forecasts.

The trend × season model fits a straight line through the deseasonalised sales of the last few years, then multiplies the seasonal index back in. A short window follows recent growth; a long window remembers older, slower years.

Prescriptive · what should we do?

Turn the forecast into a plan

Refit the chosen model on all 293 months and forecast June to November 2016. A forecast is a midpoint, so the plan adds a buffer sized from the backtest errors: buffer = forecast × z × σ, where σ is the standard deviation of the percentage errors.
