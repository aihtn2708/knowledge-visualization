Delivery Route Planning Lab

A distributor delivers to 16 stores every morning from one warehouse. Map the demand, see why simple planning wastes kilometres, compare routing methods, and choose the truck size that makes the daily round cheapest.

google/or-tools ★ about 14.3kApache-2.0 licenceC++, Python, Java, C#

OR-Tools is Google's open-source software for optimisation. This lab uses the capacitated vehicle routing example from its documentation: one depot, 16 stops on a city grid, and a demand at each stop. The best routes on this page were solved with OR-Tools.

The business question

DecisionWhich truck size to run, and which stores each truck visits in which order.
ConstraintA truck cannot carry more roll cages than its capacity, and every truck starts and ends at the warehouse.
Measure of successCost per day: a fixed cost for each truck used (driver, depreciation) plus a cost per kilometre (fuel, tolls, wear).
Data17 locations on a street grid (one block is 1.14 km east–west and 0.8 km north–south), with distances measured along the streets.

Compare routing methods

Pick a truck capacity and a method. The map above redraws the routes. Nearest neighbour drives to the closest store that still fits, which is quick to explain but greedy. The savings method (Clarke and Wright) starts with one trip per store and merges the two trips that save the most distance, again and again. OR-Tools searches far more combinations and finds the best routes it can within a time limit.
