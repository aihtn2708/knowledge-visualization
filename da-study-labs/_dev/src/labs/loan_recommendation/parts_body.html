Loan Recommendation Lab

You are an analyst at a consumer finance company. Eighty people asked for a loan online. Use each person's credit score and credit history to decide who qualifies for which product, recommend the best one and work out how much to offer. Write SQL, Python and Excel formulas right here and check each result.

The brief

Lotus Consumer Finance (a made-up company) runs a pre-screening campaign. Every applicant has a monthly income, existing monthly installments elsewhere, a requested amount and term, and a credit score from 300 to 850 (higher is safer). A blank score means the person has no credit history yet (a "thin file"). The history table lists each past loan with its worst number of days past due (max_dpd). All amounts are in VND.

The company sells four products. A person is eligible for a product when all three hold: (1) the worst max_dpd over all their past loans is 90 or less, (2) monthly_income ≥ min_income, and (3) credit_score ≥ min_score, or the score is blank and thin_file_ok = 1. The product rules, rates and limits are invented for this exercise and are not any lender's policy.

The products are listed from cheapest to most expensive (flat_rate is the yearly interest on the original amount). The recommendation is the cheapest product the person is eligible for. The offer is the largest amount that fits the product limits and the person's budget, explained in task 5.

SQL

Eligibility is a join on rules, so every applicant is compared with every product in one statement. The hard part is NULL: a missing score is neither above nor below a threshold, so you have to say what it means.

Python

The rules become a function you can read like the policy document. Adding a new rule, such as an age limit, is one more if line. The price is that you write every loop yourself.

Excel

A 0/1 grid of applicants by products lets an underwriter see why someone failed. Each step of the offer sits in its own column, so a reviewer can audit it. Changing a limit on the products sheet updates everything.

The data is generated for this lab and describes no real people. The SQL runs on SQLite, the Python on a small built-in interpreter (loops, dicts, lists, comprehensions, functions, f-strings, math and collections), and the Excel formulas on a small built-in engine. All three give the same results on these 80 applicants.

Background: the 300 to 850 score scale and the Low, Medium and High bands (300–500, 501–700, 701–850) follow SeABank's explanation of the CIC credit score. That a higher score means easier approval, lower rates and higher limits is described in Shinhan Finance's article on how scores affect borrowing. The "91 days or more is bad debt" line matches debt group 3 in Circular 11/2021/TT-NHNN. Eligibility rules, rates and limits are fictional.
