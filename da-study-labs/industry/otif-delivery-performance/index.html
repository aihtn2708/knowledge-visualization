OTIF Delivery Performance Lab

A global health supply chain shipped about 10,000 orders of medicines and test kits to more than 40 countries. Measure how often they arrived on time, find where the misses come from, predict which shipments are at risk, and set delivery promises the network can keep.

statsmodels/statsmodels ★ about 11.7kBSD-3-Clause licencePython

statsmodels is the standard Python library for statistical models. This lab uses its logistic regression to explain and predict late deliveries. The data is USAID's Supply Chain Shipment Pricing Data (the PEPFAR SCMS delivery history, 2006 to 2015), from a public GitHub mirror. Only aggregated figures and model results are shown here.

About the repository

The business question

What OTIF meansOn time in full: the share of orders delivered by the promised date and with the full quantity. A late order and a short order both count as a miss.
What this data can measureThe on-time part: each record has a scheduled and an actual delivery date. Delivered quantities are not recorded, so the in-full part is handled as an assumption in the last step.
DecisionWhich routes need attention, and how many days of buffer each route needs in its promised date to reach an OTIF target.

Predictive · which shipments are at risk?

A late-delivery model

A logistic regression, fitted with statsmodels on 70% of the orders, estimates the chance an order arrives late from its route, mode, product type, period and destination. Each bar is an odds ratio: above 1 means more likely to be late than the reference case, with its 95% confidence interval.

Risk of a new order

Prescriptive · what should we promise?

Set promise dates that hit the target

Adding buffer days to the promised date turns some late orders into on-time ones. Using each route's history of delays, find the smallest buffer that reaches the target. OTIF then also depends on the in-full rate: if shortages are unrelated to delays, OTIF ≈ on-time rate × in-full rate.
