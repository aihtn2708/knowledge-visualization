Credit Scorecard Lab

A consumer lender wants an application scorecard that a loan officer and a regulator can read line by line. Find which applicant attributes separate good from bad borrowers, turn a model into points, test it on applicants it has not seen, and choose the approval cut-off.

ShichenXie/scorecardpy ★ about 805MIT licencePython port of the R package scorecard

scorecardpy is a widely used open-source toolkit for traditional credit scorecards: weight-of-evidence binning, information value, logistic regression and points scaling. Every number on this page comes from running its README workflow on its bundled German credit data.

The business question

DecisionWhich score an applicant needs to be approved.
Why points, not a black boxEach answer on the application adds or removes points, so a declined customer can be told why, and the model can be audited.
Cost of mistakesThe dataset's own cost matrix: approving a bad borrower costs 5, declining a good one costs 1.
Data1,000 past applicants from a German bank with 20 attributes; 300 turned out bad. scorecardpy splits them into 700 for building and 300 for testing.
Descriptive · who turned out bad?

Bad rate by attribute

Binning groups each attribute into a few ranges or categories. The bad rate in each bin shows whether the attribute tells good and bad apart. Weight of evidence (WoE) puts it on one scale: ln(share of all bads in the bin ÷ share of all goods in the bin). Positive means riskier than average.

Diagnostic · which attributes matter most?

Information value ranks the attributes

Information value (IV) adds up how strongly every bin of an attribute separates goods from bads. A common rule of thumb: below 0.02 useless, 0.02 to 0.1 weak, 0.1 to 0.3 medium, above 0.3 strong. scorecardpy drops attributes below 0.02 before modelling.

Predictive · how risky is this applicant?

The scorecard

A logistic regression on the WoE values picks the attributes and their weights; scorecardpy then scales the result to points. Every applicant starts with the base points and gains or loses points for each answer. On this scale 600 points means odds of 19 goods to 1 bad, and every 50 points doubles those odds.

Prescriptive · where should the cut-off be?

Choose the approval cut-off

A higher cut-off approves fewer people and fewer bad borrowers. Total cost = cost of a bad approval × bads approved + cost of a good decline × goods declined, counted on the 300 test applicants. The 5 : 1 ratio comes from the dataset; your own business will have its own ratio.

The data is from 1994 and in Deutsche Mark, and it is small. Real scorecards are built on tens of thousands of loans, validated on a separate time period, monitored for drift, and must exclude protected attributes such as sex or nationality whatever their information value. Choosing the cut-off on the test set, as here, flatters the result; in practice use a separate validation sample.
